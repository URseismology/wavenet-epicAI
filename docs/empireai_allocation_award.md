**Note on the March 2026 date**: this letter is clearly from earlier in the award
cycle — by the time we actually engaged with Empire AI (September 2026), Beta was
already operational and we successfully ran real jobs on it (2026-09-04, see

explains *why* our access started with Alpha ("all Beta projects onboarded to Alpha
first") — this is the actual origin of the Alpha-first infrastructure sequence
documented throughout this project's Empire AI work.

**This letter only states the Beta award (3,000 SU).** No separate Alpha-specific SU
figure is mentioned here — Alpha access may be unlimited during the pilot period (see
Coldfront snapshot below), or covered by a different/unstated allocation. Not confirmed
either way.

> The solid crust beneath our continents stores clues about Earth's birth,
> plate-tectonic motion, and where valuable resources are hidden. Scientists reveal this
> hidden world with seismic imaging: they record the faint vibrations generated by
> earthquakes or human-made sources and turn those signals into pictures of underground
> layers. Existing techniques are hampered by sparse, noisy data, costly active-source
> surveys, and uneven coverage. This project will harness powerful GPU computers to

### SU formula — now fully confirmed (resolves the "not yet verified" caveat)

Direct quote from the Coldfront SU Report caption:

> Compute SU only (storage billed separately). **SU = Hrs × SU/hr × QoS multiplier** —
> SU/hr = cluster base rate (**Alpha 1 / Beta 2 / Grace 0.5 per node-hr**); ×QoS = QoS
> multiplier (**priority 2 · standard/interactive 1 · test/long 0.5 · burst free**).
> Period = calendar month (Eastern), a job is booked to the month it finished; Lifetime
> = since project start. Dedup via `sacct -X`.

This exactly matches the QoS multiplier table already in `CLAUDE.md`/`HANDOFF.md`
(test=0.5x, interactive=1.0x, standard=1.0x, long=0.5x, priority=2.0x, burst=free) —
fully cross-confirmed, no conflict. The base rates also match the 2026-09-10 workshop
Q&A (Alpha 1, Beta 2, Grace 0.5).

**One nuance worth flagging, not fully resolved**: this caption says the base rate is
"per node-hr", while the workshop Q&A phrased it as "per GPU-hour." The SU Report
table itself has no separate GPU-count column, only `Hrs` — most likely `Hrs` already
represents GPU-hours (summed across however many GPUs a job requested), making the two
framings equivalent in practice, but this hasn't been directly verified against a job
that actually requested multiple GPUs for a nonzero duration (our own logged jobs all
rounded to `0.0` hours, too short to distinguish the two interpretations numerically).
Worth a real test once we run a longer job.
